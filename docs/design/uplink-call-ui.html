Call modes & reconnecting

A call can now start as voice, switch to video if both people agree, and keep going when the network drops for a moment. Each camera and each mic can be turned off without ending the call. Every state below says what is happening in words and with an icon, never with colour alone.

Fold away moves to the top bar

The camera toggle makes a sixth key, and six don't fit a 360dp row: 5 × 49 + 64 + 5 × 13 = 374dp. So Fold away becomes a small chevron at the start of the top bar on every connected call, voice or video. That is where it points: the call folds down and away. The bottom row keeps at most five keys.

State over their video

  • Muted: a pill under their name and timer, with the mic-off icon
  • Camera off: their avatar and name replace the picture, with a pill
  • Pills sit on a dark glass fill, so they read over any frame
  • Both come from a state message they send, never guessed from traffic stopping

Reconnecting, in time

  • ~2 s with no media: "Reconnecting…" over the call
  • Up to 30 s: the call re-dials by itself; timer and log entry carry on
  • Back: the message goes, and video restarts on a keyframe
  • 30 s passed: "Connection lost", its own outcome in the log

New strings

  • Incoming voice call · Calling…
  • Muted · Camera off · Fold away
  • {name} wants to switch to video · Switch to video · Keep voice
  • Your camera turns on only if you agree. You can turn it off again at any time.
  • Asking {name} to switch to video… · Cancel · {name} kept it voice
  • Reconnecting… · The call carries on when the network is back.
  • Connection lost · Call again · Close

Motion added: the three reconnecting dots, 1.2 s loop, staggered 200 ms. Reduced motion shows them still. Changing from voice to video uses the existing answer transition: the self-view grows in from full frame and folds into its corner.

Network feedback

"Reconnecting…" says the call stalled but not why. The phone knows when it has no network at all, and when its own network is fine but nothing is coming from the other side. These frames say which side the trouble is on, and add a quiet warning for a call that still works but is struggling. Frames 10 to 12 above are unchanged.

Whose side, in time

  • ~1 s with no network on this phone: "You're offline"
  • ~2 s with no media while this phone's network is up: "Waiting for {name}…"
  • Just after this phone switches network: "Reconnecting…" (frame 10), because the side can't be told
  • Media flowing, but late or dropped: the weak pill, for whichever side it is
  • 30 s passed: "Connection lost", unchanged

This replaces "Reconnecting, in time" above.

How a side is told

  • Offline: Android's network callback reports no default network. It is a fact, not a guess.
  • Their side: our network is up and unchanged for the last few seconds, and our relay is connected
  • Weak, yours: our send queue backs up, or the round trip climbs, as QUIC measures it on this phone: to twice the best on the current path and 150 ms over it, or past 1.5 s
  • Weak, theirs: frames from them arrive late or with gaps, and our own sending is healthy
  • None of it is sent to the other phone; each side judges from what it sees

New strings

  • You're offline · The call carries on when your network is back.
  • Waiting for {name}… · Their connection dropped. The call carries on when they're back.
  • Your connection is weak · {name}'s connection is weak

No new motion: the offline and waiting frames reuse the reconnecting dots. The weak pill appears and goes without animation, and it is held for a few seconds so it does not flicker on a single late frame.

Audio & hold

Android now treats an uplink call as a call. It knows which outputs are connected (the phone's earpiece, the speaker, a Bluetooth headset, wired headphones), and it puts the call on hold when a phone call is answered over it. The Speaker key becomes an Audio key, and each phone says when the call is on hold and whose hold it is.

A video call starts on Speaker and a voice call on Phone, unless a headset is connected, which Android picks first.

Hold, in order

  • A phone call rings: Android asks the user, and answering it holds this call
  • Held: mic, camera and playout stop; the other phone is told, and shows frame 20
  • The phone call ends: Android resumes this call; the camera comes back on a keyframe
  • Hold is not a network fault: Reconnecting, Waiting and the weak pills stay hidden while either side is held

New strings

  • Audio · Phone · Speaker · Bluetooth · Wired headphones · Mute
  • On hold · You're on a phone call. {name} can't hear or see you until it ends.
  • {name} is on a phone call

No new motion. The sheet opens and closes like the video-ask sheet, and the hold scrim appears and goes without animation.

Slint feasibility

What each piece costs in the stack we already have. The two unknowns are worth spiking before the phase they belong to.

FeatureHowConfidence
Self-view → corner Slint animates geometry directly: animate x, y, width, height { duration: 620ms; easing: ease-out-quint; } on the preview element, driven by the call state. known
Swappable previews One boolean in the model; both surfaces already exist as borrowed GL textures, so swapping is which texture each element samples. known
Picture-in-picture Android's own PiP: JNI call to enterPictureInPictureMode with an aspect ratio, then Slint just redraws into a smaller surface. The UI hides everything but the remote video while onPictureInPictureModeChanged says it's active. spike
QR display Generate the matrix in Rust, draw it into an image, hand it to Slint as an Image. No new UI machinery. known
QR scan & from image The camera already gives us frames; decode in Rust off the UI thread. Reading a saved photo needs a file picker through the activity. spike
Avatars Initials on a generated color by default — no image pipeline, no storage. A photo can come later. known
Incoming call on lock screen Full-screen-intent notification from the Java side; the service starts when the call is answered, which is also what Android's background rules require. step 6 follow-up
Theme & language Colors come from one global in Slint; strings from a lookup keyed by language, chosen at runtime rather than by the system locale. known

Phases

First the calls you'd actually place, then the things that make it pleasant, then the rest.

Phase 1essential

A call you can place without reading a 64-character key aloud.

  • Contacts: add, rename, remove, with initials avatars
  • Your QR and scanning theirs
  • Incoming, outgoing and in-call screens
  • Mic, speaker, camera flip, end
  • The answer transition and preview swap
Phase 2comfort

What a call needs once it's used daily.

  • Picture-in-picture
  • Lock-screen incoming call
  • Settings: theme, language, quality
  • Diagnostics report you can send
  • Key from a saved image
Phase 3later

Trust and polish.

  • Safety-code verification, and a warning when a key changes
  • Identity rotation
  • Photo avatars
  • Call history
  • Identity key held in the Keystore

Arabic & accessibility

Arabic is a first-class language here, not a translation pass at the end.

What's free

Slint on Android draws through Skia, which shapes Arabic properly: joined forms and mixed Arabic/Latin runs in a paragraph come out right without us doing anything.

What we build

Layout mirroring isn't automatic. Rows, icons and alignment flip from one global direction flag, so every screen is laid out in terms of start and end rather than left and right from the first line of UI code.

What stays put

Keys, fingerprints and the call timer are left-to-right even in Arabic — they're codes, not prose. Numerals follow the language.

Reachable

  • Call controls at least 48dp, with real labels for TalkBack
  • Text contrast 4.5:1, chrome over video always on a scrim
  • State never carried by color alone — the mute button changes fill, not just hue
  • Respects the system text size and reduced motion
Drawn for uplink — peer-to-peer, end-to-end encrypted calling. Screens are live HTML at the tokens above, not images, so anything here can be measured.

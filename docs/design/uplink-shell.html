Uplink Shell & Identity

What a fresh install sees before it sees anything else: a mark, a splash while the endpoint comes up, a gate for the three permissions a call cannot happen without, a name and a face to call people with, and the key that is the whole account. Drawn to the tokens already locked, in the same frames the measuring probe reads.

The mark

The u-link is chosen. The two it was picked over are kept here, because why a mark was not chosen is worth as much later as why it was. All three are two-colour, need no gradient, and are a handful of SVG paths — which matters, because the same drawing has to serve a 24dp control, a 108dp adaptive icon and a monochrome themed icon.

Why the u-link

It is the only one of the three that says the product's name and its argument at the same time: the curve is a u, and its two ends are two people with a direct line between them. It also survives the adaptive-icon mask, which the diagonal in Direct does not do as comfortably — a diagonal wants the corners the circular mask cuts off.

Accent for the link, text for the peers. In the monochrome themed icon the whole mark collapses to one colour and still reads, because the shapes do the work and not the hues.

First run, in order

Splash, then permissions, then the key. The splash is not decoration: the endpoint has to reach a relay before a call can arrive, and that is the wait it covers. Everything after it happens once — except the blocked screen, which returns whenever a permission is missing.

The shell

Three tabs, always there: People is who you can call, Connect is how anyone becomes one of them, Settings is everything else. Your own identity leaves People entirely — it was never a contact, and a list of people is a poor place to keep yourself.

✓
Tapping a row opens, the two buttons call

A contact now has four things you can do to it, so the row cannot be a single button any more. Voice and video sit at the row's end, each one tap away and each a full 48dp target, and the rest of the row opens the contact — no long press, which is undiscoverable and hostile to TalkBack. Two labelled icons, not one pill, because a pill saying Call could not say which kind of call it places.

○
What this supersedes

The locked design ends People with a "Your key" row and puts Add in its header. Both go: adding lives in Connect, and your identity lives in Connect and Settings. The bar is also permanent now, where the last build hid it on People.

○
Last called earns its line

It is the only per-contact fact worth the second line, and it is already on the device — a fingerprint under every name was decoration, since the one place it matters is a caller you have not saved.

What an identity is, and what fits in one

An identity is the key plus what you call yourself. Scanning one saves a contact outright — nickname pre-filled, nothing to type. The picture is the exception, and not by choice.

Travels inCarriesWhy
The code Key + display name A QR tops out near 2.9 KB. A key is 32 bytes and a name is tens — both fit with room to spare.
A shared link Key + display name Same payload, so a code and a link save the same contact.
The connection Picture, and the name again A 256px picture is tens of kilobytes — ten times what any QR holds. It arrives on first contact and refreshes when it changes.
▲
A picture cannot go in the code

This is arithmetic, not a decision: the largest QR holds about 2,953 bytes, and a usable picture is an order of magnitude past that. So a scanned contact appears with their name and their initial, and grows a face the first time you connect. It is worth knowing that the two are not simultaneous.

○
The name arrives twice on purpose

In the code, so a contact saves without typing; and on every connection, so it is current. The one in the code seeds the nickname. The one on the wire never overwrites it.

Privacy

Two switches, both off by default in the sense that costs you nothing: your profile is shared, and anyone with your key can reach you. Turning either on trades reach for quiet.

SwitchOnOff
Share my profile Code carries your name; every call advertises name and picture. Code is key-only; calls advertise nothing. You are a fingerprint to everyone, including contacts.
Reject unknown callers A key you have not saved never rings. It is refused at signalling and written to the call log, so you can see what you missed. Unknown keys ring, shown as a claim — quoted name, fingerprint, no picture.
○
The two switches explain each other

Advertising a profile on every call only matters while unknown callers are allowed — it is what lets a stranger say who they are. With rejection on, only saved contacts get through and you already have their name. That is why they live side by side rather than in different places.

▲
Rejected is not blocked

Refusing at signalling stops the phone ringing; it does not stop someone knowing your key is reachable, because the connection is established before we can see who it is. Worth being straight about in the caption rather than implying invisibility.

A name is a claim, not an identity

The key is who someone is; a name and a picture are what they say about themselves, and anyone can say anything. Uplink has no server to check it against, so the rule is simple: your own nickname always wins, and a name you did not choose is never shown as if you had.

▲
Why this matters here more than elsewhere

Anyone who has your key can call you, and nothing stops them setting their name to a contact's. Without this rule a stranger could ring you as "Noor" and look identical to Noor. Quotation marks, the fingerprint and the chip are what make the two calls tell apart at a glance — and the stranger's picture is withheld, because a photograph is the most convincing lie of the three.

○
What it changes in the locked plan

Contacts stay key-only and nicknames stay local — nothing there moves. What is new is that a profile now travels: plan.md says avatars are initials with "no image pipeline, no storage", and this replaces that.

○
Renaming is still yours

Adding a claimed name as a contact pre-fills the nickname field with it — pre-fills, never sets. From then on it is your name for them, and if they change theirs, yours does not move.

Can someone call you right now

The chip at the top of People answers one question: could a call reach this phone at this moment. It had two answers, Online and Offline, and Offline covered three different situations. Now it has four, and each one tells you whether there is anything to do.

ChipWhenColour
OnlineA relay is connected. A relay that drops keeps the chip at Online for 2 s; it usually comes back in a fifth of that.Answer green, as now
Connecting…At start-up until the first relay answers. After a network change or a relay drop, once 2 s have passed without one.Muted
No networkAndroid has had no default network for 1 s. A switch between wifi and mobile data leaves a gap of about half a second, so a switch never shows this.Amber
OfflineA network, and no relay for 30 s. It starts counting again whenever a network comes back.Amber
○
Why wait before showing a change

The relay connection drops and comes back within a fifth of a second several times an hour, and moving from wifi to mobile data loses the network for half a second. Without the waits the chip would flicker between answers that are not true for long enough to matter.

▲
Offline is a real failure, not a slow start

One night the relay could not be reached for ten hours while the phone had a network. Connecting… for ten hours would have hidden that. After 30 s the chip stops saying it is trying and says it is not reachable.

How the gate behaves

Three permissions, asked once, in this order. The rules below are Android's, not ours.

StateWhat the app showsHow it is detected
Never asked The Permissions screen, then the system dialogs one after another. First launch, or no grant recorded.
Denied once The Blocked screen, with Try again instead of Open settings — Android will still ask. shouldShowRequestPermissionRationale is true.
Denied for good The Blocked screen with Open settings, landing on this app's page. Rationale false after a denial. ACTION_APPLICATION_DETAILS_SETTINGS, package: uri.
Returning from settings Re-checks on resume and moves on by itself. No "I've done it" button to tap. onResume → re-check → continue.
Revoked later The same Blocked screen, on the next launch or the next call. Android restarts the process when permissions change.
○
Notifications is only runtime from API 33

minSdk is 30, so on Android 11 and 12 POST_NOTIFICATIONS is granted at install and the gate silently counts it as on. The screen shows two rows there, not three.

▲
Mandatory notifications is the strong choice

Camera and microphone genuinely make a video call impossible. Notifications only stop incoming calls being announced — with them off you could still place calls, and receive them while the app is open. Blocking the whole app is defensible and simpler to explain, but it is a choice worth making on purpose. Drawn as you asked: all three required.

Motion

The locked spec allows four movements and says nothing else moves. This adds one, and only because it explains something.

MomentWhat movesTiming
Splash The link draws from one peer to the other, left to right, then the name fades up beneath it. The two dots are already there — it is the connection that arrives. 520ms · cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.3,1)
Leaving the splash Straight cut once the endpoint is up. Never a fade — a fade would read as slowness we did not have. 0ms

Reduced motion draws the link already complete. The splash never waits for the animation: if the endpoint is up in 200ms, the splash is gone in 200ms.

What this needs that we do not have

▲
A launcher icon needs a resource table

android:icon is a reference into resources.arsc, and we have no aapt2 and no resource-table writer — our axml tool emits binary XML only. Until that exists the launcher shows Android's grey placeholder, whatever we draw. The table format is the same chunk-and-string-pool machinery axml already writes, so this is a day of work, not a rewrite.

▲
So does the system splash

Android 12+ draws its own splash from windowSplashScreenBackground and windowSplashScreenAnimatedIcon in the app's theme — also resources. Without them the system shows a plain window before ours appears. The in-app splash above needs none of this and can ship first.

▲
A profile has to cross the wire

Today nothing about a person travels but their key. A name and a picture need a message in the signalling stream, storage for the peer's copy, and a rule for refreshing it. The picture wants a cap — around 256px and a few tens of kilobytes, re-encoded by us on the way in, never forwarded as the file the user picked, which carries its own metadata. Sending a hash first and the bytes only when the peer does not have that hash keeps a familiar caller from re-sending their face every call.

✓
Contacts outgrow a file — rusqlite

A contact is now a key, a nickname, an advertised name, a picture hash, a favourite flag and a last-called time, which is a table and not a JSON file. rusqlite with bundled SQLite, built through the same clang wrapper that already compiles libopus. Turso's pure-Rust SQLite was the tempting alternative and is the wrong risk here: its own maintainers call it beta and advise against anything mission-critical, and contacts are the one thing in uplink a person cannot regenerate — lose the table and every key they ever collected is gone. Pictures never go in the table: they are files in the app's private directory named by their own hash, and the row holds the hash. Private, not external — a face someone sent you has no business in the gallery, and the same rule already keeps the logs out of public storage. A hash for a filename also means the same picture is stored once however many contacts send it, and a changed picture is a new file rather than an overwrite mid-read.

▲
And it leaks a little

Anyone who can call you learns your name and sees your picture, which is more than a key alone told them. That is the cost of a friendlier incoming call, and it is worth knowing it is being spent — it cannot be taken back once someone has it.

○
Order this suggests

Draw the mark as SVG in assets/icons and ship the in-app splash, the permission gate and the first-run screens — all of that works today. The profile needs protocol work, so it lands next to the picture-decoding path rather than with the splash. Then the resource table, which buys the launcher icon, the themed monochrome icon and the system splash at once.
